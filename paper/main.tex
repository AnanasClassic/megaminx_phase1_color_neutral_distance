\begin{abstract}
We study the first phase of a multi-phase Megaminx solver in the face-turn metric.  For a fixed target corner, the phase solves that corner together with its three incident edges.  The associated phase-coordinate graph has $11{,}692{,}800$ vertices, of which exactly $159$ lie at the maximum solved-root distance of $10$.  We allow the target corner to be selected adaptively among all $20$ corners.  An exhaustive compatibility computation shows that no reachable Megaminx state places all $20$ target blocks at distance $10$: after all corner constraints are discarded, only five globally compatible edge configurations remain, and each has odd edge-permutation parity.  Conversely, we exhibit a reachable full state whose distance is exactly $9$ for every target corner.  Therefore the exact worst-case color-neutral distance is
\[
\DCN=9.
\]
Conditional on the previously reported $114$-move face-turn upper bound, this gives the corresponding conditional bound of $113$ moves.  The accompanying artifact contains complete breadth-first tables, machine-readable certificates, independent Python and C++ exhaustive enumerators, two witness-layer verifiers, a direct Schreier--Sims membership audit, and one-command verification scripts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Megaminx is the dodecahedral analogue of the Rubik's Cube.  It has $20$ movable corners and $30$ movable edges, and its complete state graph is too large for direct breadth-first search.  Practical upper bounds therefore use a chain of subgroup or quotient-space reductions, with an exact table or certified terminal procedure for each phase.

Botz developed a computational Megaminx solver based on this approach~\cite{botz-software,botz-thread}.  The shortened phase chain gives
\[
10+15+14+8+13+13+17+26=116.
\]
The last contribution originated in Whitmore's reported two-generator computation and was not independently regenerated in Botz's thesis, so $116$ was a conditional public bound~\cite{botz-software,botz-116}.  A displayed depth-$14$ row was later identified as a transcription error; the corresponding phase ends at depth $13$~\cite{botz-116-correction}.  Rokicki subsequently reported $24$-move solutions, using an enlarged generator set, for all $16$ distance-$26$ positions and then for all $7{,}595$ distance-$25$ positions, reducing the reported total first to $115$ and then to $114$~\cite{rokicki-115,rokicki-114}.  Botz later referred to $114$ as the improved upper bound~\cite{botz-114}.  These announcements form the current public record of that global bound rather than a refereed reconstruction.

\paragraph{Relation to prior computational work.}
Twisty-puzzle state spaces are concrete permutation-group actions.  Joyner gives a systematic group-theoretic treatment of the Rubik's Cube and related puzzles, while Xu describes the corresponding Megaminx model~\cite{joyner2008,xu2018}.  The closest computational precedents are certified Rubik's Cube bounds: Kunkle and Cooperman obtained a $26$-move upper bound through large-scale coset computation, and Rokicki, Kociemba, Davidson, and Dethridge later proved the exact diameter $20$ using subgroup decomposition, symmetry, and exhaustive search~\cite{kunkle2007,rokicki2013}.  The fixed-target table below is also a pattern database in the sense of Korf and of Culberson and Schaeffer~\cite{korf1997,culberson1998}.  The new issue here is the compatibility of twenty overlapping phase coordinates when the target is chosen adaptively.

The original first phase chooses one corner and solves that corner together with its three incident edges.  We formalize the freedom to choose the most favorable corner and prove that it reduces the exact worst-case phase cost from $10$ to $9$.

The contribution is deliberately narrow:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item we reproduce the complete fixed-target distance distribution;
  \item we prove the color-neutral upper bound $9$ by an exhaustive relaxed CSP and a parity obstruction;
  \item we certify a reachable state attaining distance $9$ for all twenty targets;
  \item we state the resulting conditional one-move improvement to the externally reported global bound.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Megaminx states and metric}

A Megaminx face turn cyclically permutes the five corners and five edges incident with one face.  We use the \emph{face-turn metric}: each nonzero power of a face turn, namely $72^\circ$, $144^\circ$, $216^\circ$, or $288^\circ$, costs one move.  Thus the metric alphabet contains $48$ moves.

Corner pieces and positions are indexed by $\{0,\ldots,19\}$ and edge pieces and positions by $\{0,\ldots,29\}$.  Corner orientations lie in $\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$ and edge orientations in $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$.  Every face turn induces an even corner permutation and an even edge permutation, and preserves the total orientation sums.  The standard reachability characterization states that these conditions are also sufficient~\cite[Theorems 3.8 and 3.12]{xu2018}.  For the upper bound below, only even edge parity is needed.  Reachability of the lower-bound witness is certified directly by a separate Schreier--Sims membership computation; the usual parity and orientation conditions provide an additional consistency check.

\section{The fixed-target first phase}

Fix a target corner $v$ and let $e_1,e_2,e_3$ be its incident target edges.  A phase coordinate records
\[
(c,o_c,p_1,p_2,p_3,o_1,o_2,o_3),
\]
where $c,o_c$ are the target-corner position and orientation, and $p_i,o_i$ are the position and orientation of edge $e_i$.  Since the edge positions are distinct, the coordinate space has
\begin{align*}
|X|&=20\cdot3\cdot(30\cdot29\cdot28)\cdot2^3\\
&=11{,}692{,}800
\end{align*}
vertices.  This agrees with the coordinate allocation and ranking formula in the publicly available first-phase implementation~\cite{botz-12gen}.

The phase-coordinate graph $\Gamma_v$ is generated by all $48$ FTM moves.  Complete breadth-first search from the solved target coordinate gives \cref{tab:distribution}.  Every coordinate is visited exactly once.  All twenty target choices have the same distribution, and a full regeneration from source produces certificate files byte-identical to the distributed versions.

\begin{remark}[Solved-root eccentricity versus graph diameter]
The full Megaminx state graph is a Cayley graph, but $\Gamma_v$ is a Schreier graph for the action on the tracked-block coordinates.  Its stabilizer is not normal, so the coordinate space is not a quotient group and the graph need not be vertex-transitive.  Indeed, an exhaustive orbit-reduced computation gives
\[
\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma_v)=11,
\]
whereas the solved coordinate has eccentricity $10$.  The checker normalizes orientations and tracked-edge labels, reduces $81{,}200$ positional roots by $120$ spatial symmetries to $708$ orbit representatives, and performs complete BFS from every representative.  Of these representatives, $637$ have eccentricity $10$ and $71$ have eccentricity $11$.  These are orbit-representative counts, not vertex counts.  The ordinary diameter is not used in the color-neutral proof.
\end{remark}

\section{Color-neutral distance}

Let $V$ be the set of twenty target corners.  For a reachable full state $g$, let $q_v(g)$ be its local coordinate for target $v$ and let $d_v(g)$ be the distance from that coordinate to the solved target coordinate.

\begin{definition}
The color-neutral first-phase distance and its worst-case value are
\[
\dCN(g)=\min_{v\in V}d_v(g),
\qquad
\DCN=\max_{g}\dCN(g),
\]
where the maximum ranges over reachable full Megaminx states.
\end{definition}

Since the solved coordinate has eccentricity $10$ for every fixed target, $\DCN\le10$.  The question is whether a legal full state can lie at depth $10$ for all twenty targets simultaneously.

\section{Upper bound: no simultaneous depth-10 state}

For each target $v$, let $A_v$ be its set of $159$ depth-$10$ coordinates.  Assume for contradiction that a reachable state $g$ satisfies $d_v(g)=10$ for all $v$.  Then $q_v(g)\in A_v$ for every target.

\subsection{Edge-signature relaxation}

Identify each target piece with its home position in the solved labeling.  Let $E$ be the set of thirty edge pieces, let $P_E=\{0,\ldots,29\}$ be the edge positions, and let $E(v)$ be the three target edges incident with $v$.  Forgetting the target corner defines
\[
\sigma_v:A_v\longrightarrow
\bigl(P_E\times\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\bigr)^{E(v)}.
\]
Its image $S_v=\sigma_v(A_v)$ contains exactly $80$ distinct edge signatures for every target, although $|A_v|=159$.

The relaxed CSP has one variable $s_v\in S_v$ per target and accepts a selection exactly when:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item for every dodecahedral edge $e=uv$, the endpoint signatures agree, $s_u(e)=s_v(e)$;
  \item the thirty assigned edge positions are pairwise distinct, and hence form a permutation of $P_E$.
\end{enumerate}
Corner information and all reachability constraints are discarded.

\begin{lemma}[Soundness of the relaxation]\label{lem:relaxation-sound}
If a full Megaminx state has distance $10$ for all twenty targets, then its projected signatures solve the relaxed CSP.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each local coordinate belongs to $A_v$, so its signature belongs to $S_v$.  A physical edge has one position and orientation independently of which endpoint block observes it, giving endpoint agreement.  Finally, the edge positions of a full state form a permutation.  Thus both CSP constraints hold.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exhaustive enumeration}

The direct enumerator maintains a partial choice of signatures, the values assigned to shared edges, and the owner of every occupied position.  A signature is viable precisely when it agrees with all assigned shared edges and does not reuse a position already owned by another edge.

\begin{lstlisting}
Search(partial assignment):
  if all 20 blocks are assigned:
      output the induced edge assignment; return
  choose an unassigned block with the fewest viable signatures
  if that block has no viable signature: return
  for every viable signature of that block:
      assign it; Search(partial assignment); undo it
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lemma}[Completeness of the enumeration]\label{lem:csp-complete}
The backtracking search outputs every solution of the relaxed CSP.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a CSP solution extending the current partial assignment.  Its value for the selected block is viable: disagreement on a shared edge violates the first constraint, and reuse of an occupied position by another edge violates the second.  Since the algorithm branches over every viable value, one branch preserves the fixed solution.  Induction on the number of unassigned blocks reaches it.  If the selected block has no viable value, no CSP solution can extend the current partial assignment.  Hence no solution is omitted.
\end{proof}

Two independent implementations enumerate the same finite problem.  The C++ implementation follows the direct search above with dynamic minimum-domain selection.  The Python implementation instead combines AC-3 consistency on shared edges, singleton propagation for the all-different constraint, and minimum-remaining-values branching.  They return the same five complete assignments.

Every resulting edge permutation has cycle type $2^3 3^8$: three transpositions and eight $3$-cycles.  Each is therefore odd.  The five permutations are listed in \cref{app:five-configurations} and distributed as machine-readable certificates.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:edge-parity}
Every reachable Megaminx state has an even edge permutation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A face turn acts on edges as a $5$-cycle, whose sign is $(-1)^{5-1}=+1$.  Every generator and hence every product of generators is even.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:upper}
$\DCN\le9$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A reachable state at depth $10$ for all targets would yield a relaxed-CSP solution by \cref{lem:relaxation-sound}.  The complete enumeration of \cref{lem:csp-complete} leaves exactly five possibilities, all with odd edge permutation, contradicting \cref{lem:edge-parity}.  Therefore every reachable state has at least one target at distance at most $9$.
\end{proof}

\section{Lower bound: a reachable state with twenty distances equal to nine}

Let $g_\star$ be the full cubie state in \cref{app:witness}.  Its reachability is certified directly as follows.  An independent verifier represents the twelve face generators as permutations of $120$ oriented cubie-position states, constructs the generated group by Schreier--Sims, obtains order
\[
\frac{20!}{2}3^{19}\,\frac{30!}{2}2^{29},
\]
and confirms that the permutation representing $g_\star$ belongs to this group.  As an independent consistency check, the witness also has even corner and edge permutations and zero total corner and edge orientations.

For each target $v$, two separately written verifiers form $q_v(g_\star)$ and check that it occurs in the union of the depth-$9$ and depth-$10$ certificate sets but not in the depth-$10$ set.  Consequently
\[
(d_v(g_\star))_{v\in V}=(9,9,9,9,9,9,9,9,9,9,9,9,9,9,9,9,9,9,9,9).
\]

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lower}
$\DCN\ge9$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The directly certified reachable witness $g_\star$ satisfies $\dCN(g_\star)=9$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The exact worst-case color-neutral distance of the first corner-block phase is
\[
\boxed{\DCN=9}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Combine \cref{prop:upper,prop:lower}.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequence for the global upper bound}

Choosing the target does not add a physical move; it selects a reference frame.  Let
\[
G=G_0\supset G_1\supset\cdots\supset G_k=\{1\}
\]
be a canonical phase chain whose first target is one fixed corner block.  If a physical rotation $\rho$ maps that block to a target $v$, then $G_i^{(v)}=\rho G_i\rho^{-1}$ is a conjugate chain.  Rotations permute the twelve face generators and preserve FTM length, so all later worst-case guarantees are unchanged.

\begin{corollary}[Conditional on the reported $114$-move computation]\label{cor:113}
If the external computations underlying the reported $114$-move FTM upper bound are valid under the same move convention and phase-chain semantics, then
\[
\operatorname{diam}_{\FTM}(\mathrm{Megaminx})\le113.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Select a target attaining $\dCN(g)$, solve the first phase in at most nine moves by \cref{thm:main}, and continue through the conjugated chain.  This replaces the fixed first-phase contribution $10$ by $9$ while preserving every later contribution, giving $114-1=113$.
\end{proof}

The theorem $\DCN=9$ is independently certified by the accompanying artifact.  The number $113$ remains conditional because the artifact does not regenerate the much larger external computation underlying the reported $114$-move bound.

\section{Conclusion}

The solved coordinate of the fixed first-block phase has eccentricity $10$, but the twenty depth-$10$ constraints are mutually incompatible in every reachable full state.  A relaxed edge-only CSP leaves five candidates, all excluded by edge parity.  A directly certified reachable state at distance $9$ from every target proves sharpness.  Hence the exact color-neutral first-phase distance is $9$.  Conditional on the external $114$-move computation, this lowers the corresponding global bound to $113$.

\section{Reachability consistency conditions}\label{sec:reachability-note}

The standard cubie-coordinate characterization states that a state is reachable precisely when
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{sgn}(\pi_C)&=+1,& \sum_{i=0}^{19}o_C(i)&=0\pmod3,\\
\operatorname{sgn}(\pi_E)&=+1,& \sum_{i=0}^{29}o_E(i)&=0\pmod2.
\end{align*}
The witness satisfies all four conditions.  The lower-bound proof does not depend solely on this cited characterization: direct membership of the witness in the group generated by the twelve face turns is checked independently by Schreier--Sims, together with equality of the computed group order and the invariant-compatible state count.
